## Methods

### Cross-panel convergence analysis

To determine whether the panel-specific composite findings represented isolated abnormalities or a coordinated systemic immune-remodeling architecture, one principal integrated remodeling score was selected from each of the ten cytometry panels (CP7, CP8, CP10, CP16, CP22, CP23, CP24, CP25, CP26, and CP28). In the full cohort, each score was residualized for age, sex, and disease group, and pairwise associations between the residualized scores were quantified using Spearman correlation. The same analysis was repeated separately in participants with cancer and healthy controls after adjustment for age and sex. Pairwise sample sizes were retained for all estimates, and Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the 45 principal-score comparisons. The full 66-score correlation screen was considered supplementary because several component scores shared partially overlapping biological features and were therefore not statistically independent.

### Immunotype association analysis

The five previously defined peripheral immunotypes (G1_Naive, G2_Primed, G3_Progressive, G4_Chronic, and G5_Suppressive) were biologically resolved using the ten principal integrated remodeling scores. For each score, a multivariable linear model was fitted with immunotype as the main factor and age, sex, and disease group as covariates. Heteroskedasticity-consistent HC3 covariance estimates were used. Omnibus immunotype effects were corrected across the ten principal scores using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, and post-hoc pairwise contrasts were corrected within each score. Internal reproducibility was assessed by repeating the models independently in the original training and validation subsets. Cancer-only sensitivity models additionally adjusted for cancer subgroup, and a further sensitivity analysis included recorded treatment-status category.

### Principal component analysis

Principal component analysis was used to determine whether the ten integrated scores formed a lower-dimensional systemic remodeling structure. In the primary PCA, each score was residualized for age and sex, standardized, and entered into the decomposition. A second PCA was performed after additional residualization for disease group to determine whether the global structure persisted beyond the cancer-versus-healthy mean shift. Component signs were oriented such that higher PC1 values represented increasing multi-compartment remodeling and higher PC2 values represented relative predominance of myeloid, monocyte/macrophage-like, and NK remodeling over CD8/T–NK and regulatory-checkpoint remodeling. Immunotype centroids and their confidence intervals were calculated in the disease-adjusted PCA space. Multivariate variance partitioning was evaluated with 4,999 permutations. Internal stability was assessed by fitting separate PCA models in the training and validation subsets, comparing component loadings and principal subspaces, and projecting validation participants using the training-derived solution.

### Unsupervised clustering and stability analysis

Unsupervised analyses were performed to test whether the disease-adjusted ten-score space supported additional discrete remodeling subtypes. Candidate solutions from k = 2 to k = 8 were evaluated using k-means and Ward hierarchical clustering, with diagonal Gaussian-mixture information criteria used as a supplementary model-selection check. Cluster tendency was assessed using the Hopkins statistic. Internal validity was evaluated using silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz, and Davies–Bouldin indices. Stability was assessed by repeated 80% subsampling and by fitting independent solutions in the training and validation subsets. Adjusted Rand index and cluster-level Jaccard similarity were used to quantify reproducibility. This analysis was prespecified as a model-selection and anti-overclaiming procedure rather than as a basis for assigning new clinical subtype labels.

## Results

### Cross-panel convergence reveals a coordinated systemic remodeling architecture

To determine whether the panel-specific findings represented isolated associations or a coordinated systemic architecture, cross-panel convergence was evaluated at the subject level. Thirty-nine of the 45 pairwise comparisons among the ten principal integrated scores remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction. The strongest convergence was observed between CP24 CD8 differentiation remodeling and CP28 T/NK-interface remodeling (rho = 0.563, qFDR = 2.67 × 10^-69), between CP8 CD4 helper/regulatory remodeling and CP25 CD4 regulatory-checkpoint remodeling (rho = 0.487, qFDR = 1.32 × 10^-49), and between CP7 checkpoint remodeling and CP28 T/NK-interface remodeling (rho = 0.466, qFDR = 5.80 × 10^-45). Myeloid convergence was also evident between CP10 myeloid/granulocytic remodeling and CP23 monocyte/macrophage-like remodeling (rho = 0.370, qFDR = 2.21 × 10^-27), whereas CP16 APC/DC-like remodeling correlated with CP23 monocyte/macrophage-like remodeling (rho = 0.239, qFDR = 7.53 × 10^-12). Additional within-domain associations included CP7–CP24 CD8 remodeling (rho = 0.261, qFDR = 6.04 × 10^-14) and CP26–CP28 NK/T–NK-interface remodeling (rho = 0.256, qFDR = 1.69 × 10^-13). All prespecified cross-panel associations retained the same positive direction in the cancer-only and healthy-only analyses, indicating that the observed convergence was not attributable solely to the cancer-versus-healthy group separation.

### Integrated remodeling scores biologically resolve the five peripheral immunotypes

To biologically resolve the five previously defined peripheral immunotypes, one principal integrated remodeling score from each cytometry panel was modeled as a function of immunotype after adjustment for age, sex, and disease group. Immunotype was associated with all ten remodeling axes after false-discovery-rate correction, with the largest incremental contributions observed for T/NK-interface, NK, myeloid/granulocytic, and CD4 helper/regulatory remodeling. The adjusted profiles formed biologically distinct rather than uniformly ordered states. G1_Naive occupied the lowest remodeling position across most axes and therefore represented the low-remodeling reference configuration. G2_Primed remained close to G1 but showed an early increase dominated by CD4 helper/regulatory remodeling. G3_Progressive exhibited intermediate CD4 regulatory, humoral, and myeloid remodeling. G4_Chronic was distinguished by the highest CD8 differentiation and T/NK-interface remodeling, including marked elevations in CP24 and CP28 scores. In contrast, G5_Suppressive showed the strongest myeloid/granulocytic, monocyte/macrophage-like, APC/DC-like, and NK remodeling, together with broader CD4 and checkpoint-associated changes. Thus, G4 and G5 represented two advanced but biologically distinct configurations: a CD8/T–NK-oriented chronic-effector state and a myeloid/monocyte/NK-oriented suppressive state, respectively.

All ten omnibus immunotype associations were reproduced independently in the training and validation subsets. Among the 40 prespecified G1-referenced contrasts, 21 remained FDR-significant with the same direction in the full, training, and validation analyses. The immunotype effects also remained significant in cancer-only models adjusted for age, sex, and cancer subgroup. Additional adjustment for treatment status had negligible effects on the estimated pairwise contrasts, with a median absolute change of 0.002 score units and a maximum absolute change of 0.015, indicating that the observed immunotype-remodeling profiles were not materially explained by recorded treatment exposure.

### Principal components identify a global remodeling gradient and divergent advanced configurations

Principal component analysis was used to determine whether the ten integrated panel scores formed a lower-dimensional systemic structure. After age and sex adjustment, the first principal component accounted for 35.6% of total score variance and showed uniformly positive contributions from adaptive, humoral, innate-cytotoxic, and myeloid axes, supporting its interpretation as a broad multi-compartment remodeling gradient. The second component accounted for 15.8% of variance and contrasted myeloid/granulocytic, monocyte/macrophage-like, and NK remodeling with CD8 differentiation, T/NK-interface, and regulatory-checkpoint remodeling. This two-axis structure remained after additional removal of the cancer-versus-healthy mean shift, with PC1 and PC2 explaining 30.5% and 16.6% of disease-adjusted variance, respectively.

Immunotype accounted for 11.7% of the disease-adjusted multivariate score variance in permutation analysis (P = 0.0002). The adjusted immunotype centroids were ordered along PC1 from G1_Naive and G2_Primed through G3_Progressive to G4_Chronic and G5_Suppressive, consistent with increasing global remodeling. However, G4 and G5 occupied opposing PC2 positions: G4 was shifted toward the CD8/T–NK pole, whereas G5 was shifted toward the myeloid/monocyte/NK pole. The G5–G4 difference on PC2 was 2.325 score units (P < 1 × 10^-20), providing a compact systems-level explanation for why both immunotypes represented advanced remodeling yet differed biologically.

Separate training and validation analyses reproduced the variance contribution and loading structure of the first two components. PC1 loading cosine similarity was 0.953 and PC2 loading cosine similarity was 0.884 after sign alignment. When validation participants were projected using the training-derived PCA solution, the training-versus-validation correlations of immunotype centroids were 0.984 for PC1 and 0.977 for PC2. Despite these stable centroid differences, silhouette values were negative, indicating substantial individual-level overlap. The immunotypes therefore occupied distinct mean positions within a continuous remodeling space rather than forming five non-overlapping classes in the compressed ten-score representation.

Unsupervised clustering supported this interpretation. Although the score space showed non-random structure (mean Hopkins statistic = 0.757), the only highly stable solution was k = 2 and primarily divided the continuous PC1 axis into low- and high-remodeling groups; PC1 alone recovered this solution with an AUC of 0.9997. A biologically interpretable three-cluster solution separated low-remodeling, CD8/T–NK-oriented, and myeloid/monocyte/NK-oriented configurations, but its silhouette coefficient was low and training–validation assignment reproducibility was insufficient for subtype claims. Accordingly, the integrated score space did not support additional sharply discrete and reproducible remodeling subtypes.

## Discussion

The additional systems-level analyses extend the panel-specific findings in three complementary ways. First, the cross-panel convergence analysis demonstrates that the major remodeling scores are not statistically isolated. The strongest associations occurred between independently designed panels interrogating overlapping biological domains, including CD8 differentiation and the T/NK interface across CP7, CP24, and CP28; CD4 helper/regulatory and regulatory-checkpoint remodeling across CP8 and CP25; and myeloid, APC/DC-like, and monocyte/macrophage-like remodeling across CP10, CP16, and CP23. Importantly, these relationships persisted after adjustment for disease group in the full cohort and remained directionally concordant within both the cancer and healthy strata. The resulting pattern therefore cannot be explained solely by parallel mean shifts between patients with cancer and healthy controls. Instead, it supports a coordinated systemic architecture in which adaptive, humoral, innate-cytotoxic, and myeloid remodeling axes vary together across individuals.

Second, mapping the composite remodeling framework onto the original five immunotypes provided an interpretable biological decomposition of the cluster labels. The profiles did not represent a simple monotonic sequence from G1 to G5. Rather, two advanced but distinct remodeling configurations emerged. G4_Chronic was dominated by CD8 differentiation, terminal-effector/checkpoint, and T/NK-interface remodeling, whereas G5_Suppressive was characterized by myeloid/granulocytic and monocyte/macrophage-like remodeling together with marked NK and broader multi-compartment changes. G2_Primed and G3_Progressive occupied intermediate positions, with progressively stronger CD4 helper/regulatory, humoral, and myeloid remodeling. These relationships remained after demographic and disease-group adjustment and were reproduced across the original training and validation partitions, supporting the internal stability of the biological mapping.

Third, PCA provided a compact systems-level bridge between cross-panel convergence and the immunotype profiles. A single broad component captured coordinated variation across all ten panels, indicating that diverse immune abnormalities share a common systemic remodeling dimension. This general axis was not sufficient to explain the full architecture, however. A second, internally reproducible component separated CD8/T–NK-oriented remodeling from myeloid/monocyte/NK-oriented remodeling and thereby distinguished G4_Chronic from G5_Suppressive despite their similarly elevated global remodeling positions. The immune architecture is therefore better represented as a shared global remodeling gradient combined with alternative biological configurations than as a single unidimensional sequence.

The unsupervised analyses further supported a continuous rather than sharply categorical interpretation. The only highly stable clustering solution dichotomized overall remodeling burden and was almost completely recoverable from PC1 alone. Although a three-state solution qualitatively recapitulated low-remodeling, CD8/T–NK-oriented, and myeloid/NK-oriented regions of the PCA space, it lacked sufficient cross-cohort reproducibility to justify new subtype labels. Avoiding additional cluster nomenclature is therefore important, because the principal components and original immunotypes capture the underlying organization more faithfully than artificial boundaries imposed on overlapping immune configurations.

Several interpretive boundaries should be retained. The original immunotypes and the present remodeling scores were derived from the same underlying SDY2583 flow-cytometry resource; therefore, the present analyses represent biological resolution, dimensional decomposition, and internal split-sample replication rather than independent external validation. Correlations and principal components do not establish causality, prognosis, treatment response, or temporal progression. The G1-to-G5 ordering along PC1 should be described as an adjusted centroid gradient rather than a demonstrated longitudinal trajectory. In addition, partial overlap among composite-score components and shared technical covariance may contribute to some cross-panel associations. Nevertheless, the reproducible organization across panels and internal subsets indicates that multi-compartment remodeling summaries provide a more coherent translational framework than isolated marker-level abnormalities.
