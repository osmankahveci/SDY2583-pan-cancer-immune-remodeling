# Bootstrap component-stability analysis

## Methods

### Bootstrap component stability

Internal stability of the integrated remodeling architecture was evaluated using 2,000 nonparametric bootstrap resamples drawn with replacement within disease-group strata. The analysis used the common complete-case population of 829 participants with valid age, sex, immunotype, and all 66 composite-score values. For each resample, the entire dimensional-analysis pipeline was repeated: each of the 10 principal integrated scores was residualized for age, sex, and disease group, the residualized scores were standardized, and principal component analysis was refitted. The first two bootstrap components were matched to the reference solution by maximizing absolute loading congruence and were sign-aligned to the reference orientation. Stability was quantified using component-wise loading cosine congruence, canonical correlations for the two-component subspace, bootstrap percentile intervals for individual loadings, explained-variance distributions, and correlations between reference and bootstrap immunotype centroids.

The cancer-versus-healthy effect of every composite score was additionally re-estimated in each bootstrap sample using age- and sex-adjusted linear models with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied separately across the 10 principal integrated scores and across the full 66-score catalogue. For each score, stability was summarized as the proportion of bootstrap samples retaining the reference effect direction, the proportion remaining FDR-significant, and the 95% percentile interval of the adjusted cancer coefficient.

## Results

### Bootstrap resampling confirms the stability of the systemic remodeling dimensions

The two-dimensional systemic architecture was highly stable across 2,000 disease-stratified bootstrap resamples. PC1 loading cosine congruence had a median of 0.998 (95% bootstrap interval, 0.994–1.000), whereas PC2 congruence had a median of 0.994 (0.982–0.998). The minimum canonical correlation between the reference and bootstrap two-component subspaces was 0.994 (0.982–0.998). PC1 explained a median of 30.6% of variance (95% interval, 28.5–32.5%), and PC2 explained 16.7% (15.5–18.0%). All 10 PC1 loadings remained positive in 100% of resamples, and the prespecified myeloid/monocyte/NK-versus-CD8/T–NK orientation of PC2 was retained in 100%. Bootstrap intervals excluded zero for all PC1 loadings and for 9 of 10 PC2 loadings; only the comparatively small CP8 loading on PC2 crossed zero, indicating that CP8 contributed primarily to the shared global-remodeling dimension rather than to the second-axis contrast. Immunotype centroid positions were also highly reproducible, with median bootstrap correlations of 0.995 (0.972–1.000) for PC1 and 0.995 (0.977–1.000) for PC2.

At the score level, 62 of 66 components (93.9%) retained the reference cancer-versus-healthy direction in at least 95% of bootstrap samples, and the 95% bootstrap coefficient interval excluded zero for the same 62 components. Fifty-six of 66 scores (84.8%) remained FDR-significant in at least 80% of resamples. The four components without stable non-zero bootstrap intervals were the CP24 PD-1-associated terminal score, the CP10 CCR3/eosinophil-like granulocytic score, and the two CP28 NK-like component scores, consistent with their comparatively weak primary effects. In contrast, all 10 principal integrated scores retained the positive reference direction in 98.8%–100% of resamples and had bootstrap intervals excluding zero. Nine principal scores remained FDR-significant in 99.9%–100% of resamples. The weaker CP24 integrated score retained direction in 98.8% of resamples and had a positive bootstrap interval (0.019–0.206), but remained FDR-significant in 61.4%, indicating greater multiplicity sensitivity than the other principal axes.

## Discussion addition

Bootstrap resampling provides an additional internal reproducibility check beyond split-sample replication and threshold sensitivity. The near-unit loading congruence and subspace correlations indicate that the global remodeling gradient and the myeloid-versus-CD8/T–NK configuration axis were not driven by a small number of influential participants. The greater stability of the prespecified integrated scores relative to several weak individual component scores supports the use of composite biological axes for the main narrative while retaining component-level heterogeneity in the Supplementary Results. The comparatively lower FDR stability of CP24 should be reported transparently and interpreted as evidence of a weaker, although directionally reproducible, integrated effect. As with the other internal validation analyses, bootstrap stability does not constitute independent external validation and cannot determine transportability to other cohorts, platforms, or gating implementations.
